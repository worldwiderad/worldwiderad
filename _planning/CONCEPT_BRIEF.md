## The piece (the site will host its recording and every note from its MIDI)
A 9 min 53 s orchestral piece, 48 instrument tracks + conductor track, 19,197 notes, recording and MIDI line up exactly (593.3 s). Working title unknown (file called "4part"). Instruments in score order: ornamental flute, 2 flutes, 2 oboes, bass oboe, 2 clarinets, bass clarinet, 2 bassoons (11 woodwinds); 6 horns; 3 trumpets, 5 trombones, 2 tubas; timpani, drum set, bongos, glockenspiel, 2 xylophones, snare, triangle, bass drums, cymbal (10 percussion); 2 harp, 2 synth ("Kinetic Metal", "Mystic Vibes"), 2 piano; violins 1, violins 2, violas, cellos, basses.

Structure, from the MIDI's own tempo, meter and key changes plus note density (active tracks):
- Part I 0:00–1:39. C, 4/4, 100 bpm; a sudden 2/2 burst at 160 bpm at 0:25–0:32; 30–43 tracks busy. Rising scale runs in flutes/violins (e.g. A–B–C#–D–E–F#–G#–A at 0:08). A horn call at 0:50: long-short repeated notes stepping up B–C#–D then dropping to F#, harmonised in horns, over pulsing basses.
- Part II 1:39–4:22. Key to Eb at 1:39: the orchestra drops to ~8 tracks and three stacked ostinati start together — violas and 2nd winds rock a minor third, 1st violins and 1st winds rock a fourth, cellos/bass oboe/bass clarinet rock an octave. Key to D at 2:47. Builds to a 43-track tutti at 3:00–3:15 where the horn call returns (2:57–3:13), then thins.
- Part III 4:22–6:13. 3/4 at 60 bpm; only 3 tracks sounding at first (the quietest stretch of the piece); keys F (4:50), Bb (5:26); tempo to 80 at 5:52.
- Part IV 6:13–9:53. 4/4, keys D (6:13), Eb (6:46), C# (7:38), F (8:08); long sparse stretch (4–12 tracks) with brief 5/4 bars; a rising arpeggio figure (up a 4th, a major 3rd, a minor 3rd, a 4th) stated by piano ~8:00, then the final tutti from 8:45 (38 tracks) where horns and piano take the arpeggio and the Part I scale run returns (1:19 → 8:47); fades out in 5/4 after 9:38.

## What already exists (mockup)
Hero: every note drawn as short horizontal marks, one row per instrument in score order, section labels (Woodwinds, Horns, Brass, Percussion, Harp/synth/piano, Strings) with score brackets, minutes along the bottom; a "Listen" control loads the recording on click; a blue playhead moves and played notes turn blue; clicking seeks. Typeface Petrona (the face of his essays). Black ink = done, one royal blue ink (#2340a8) = in motion / still ahead. Page is otherwise plain text entries. Radek's feedback: "It can be more distinctive. You avoided looking like AI by making no decisions. Make decisions, even with risk. Make it cooler: make a timeline along the notes, explore the actual motifs so it matches. Make something truly unique and amazing."

## Hard constraints
- Must not look or read AI-generated. Banned: purple/blue/neon gradients, gradient text, glassmorphism, glowing blobs/orbs/particles, cream background + serif display + terracotta, near-black + dot grid + acid accent, bento grids, identical card rows, tech-badge walls, emoji icons, "Hi I'm" heroes, availability pills, animated counters, typewriter headlines, fade-up-on-scroll everywhere, motion without reason, tracked all-caps eyebrow labels, "01/02/03" numbering of non-sequences, middle-dot meta strings, arrows appended to links. Copy: no em dashes, no "not X but Y", no groups of three for rhythm, no rhetorical questions answered, no hype words.
- Static site on GitHub Pages (plain HTML/CSS/vanilla JS, optionally Jekyll data files). No frameworks, no heavy libraries. Everything readable without JS. Accessible (keyboard, screen reader text equivalents, prefers-reduced-motion). Works at 360 px wide with no horizontal page scroll. Fast (no autoplay; audio loads on request).
- Honest: nothing invented; a timeline may only use real dates above (items without dates must be marked approximate or omitted).
