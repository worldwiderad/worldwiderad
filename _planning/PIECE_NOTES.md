# 4part: what the MIDI shows

All of this is read from the MIDI (`4part.mid`, 48 instrument tracks plus a conductor track, 593 s). Times are in the recording. Bars count from the MIDI's own time signatures. Everything the homepage says about the piece should match this file; if the score itself says otherwise, the score wins, so tell me.

## Where the parts divide

The piece stops completely three times before the end, and each silence is a part boundary:

| Silence | Length | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1:37.5 to 1:39.3 | 1.8 s | Part II, bar 44, key change to three flats |
| 4:20.7 to 4:24.5 | 3.8 s (the longest) | Part III, bar 112, 3/4 at ♩=60 |
| 6:12.8 to 6:15.2 | 2.4 s | Part IV, bar 151, two sharps, 4/4 |

The ♩=80 at 5:52.5 (bar 142) sits inside Part III. There, harp chords and a rising violin line lead towards the last silence.

## The material, part by part

### Part I (0:00 to 1:39)

- **Horn call (0:02.4).** All six horns with a trumpet and a trombone play a long E, a quick double E, D, E, then a fast C–B–A onto a long A.
  - Varied at 0:09.6. Restated at 0:14.4, with a continuation in the clarinet.
  - The trumpets play it against the chorale at 0:33, 0:37 and 0:42.
  - Transposed up to A♭ for the climax at 1:20 and 1:30 (clarinet, trumpets).
- **Scale run (0:09).** A fast rising scale in flutes, oboes, clarinet and violins, landing on the second horn call.
  - Again at 0:13.8 and 0:30.6 (violins), 0:59 (harp), 1:08, 1:19.5 and 1:29.
  - Not heard again until 8:46.5, where it leads into the finale.
- **Other material.**
  - 0:24.6: a repeated-note trumpet fanfare in the ♩=160 bars.
  - 0:33 to 0:48: a chorale in the woodwinds, horns and violins.
  - 0:51: a lyrical melody in violins and clarinet over repeated-note chords in the horns. The old site called these chords the horn call, which was wrong.

### Part II (1:39 to 4:21)

- **Rocking figure (1:39.3).** The strings rock between two notes in pairs (C♯ A♭ A♭ C♯ …) under a harp chord. It passes to the woodwinds and piano at 2:15, and the vibes take it in D from 3:17 to 4:00. Over it:
  - an oboe and clarinet melody at 1:58;
  - woodwind textures at 2:15 to 2:45.
- **Horn call in D (2:51).** Trumpets and trombones turn the call's head (a long note, then quick repeated notes) into a broad major-key tune: F♯, F♯F♯F♯, G, F♯, D, E, B, A. The horns take it in the full tutti at 3:03.
- **Ending.** A long flute and violin melody in D at 3:50, then a thinning texture into the longest silence.

### Part III (4:24 to 6:12)

- **4:24.** It opens with three instruments, vibes, glockenspiel and xylophone, playing a slow descending line at ♩=60.
- **4:50.** The horns play a slow chromatic descent.
- **5:21 to 5:48.** A bassoon line, then flute arpeggios.
- **5:52.5.** At ♩=80, harp chords and a rising violin line.

### Part IV (6:15 to the end)

- **The theme.** It has a fixed rhythm: long, short, even, even. The melody steps down, back up, then leaps.
  - 6:18.75, horns, chromatic (E E♭ E A A♭). Echoed by the trumpets at 6:27.75.
  - 6:49.5, flute, fuller (F E♭ F A♭ C♯ C C♯ F♯ F). Answered by the oboe at 6:58.5.
  - 8:47.25, full orchestra in D major (F♯ E F♯ A D C♯ D G F♯). Again at 9:05 in the strings, and one last time on the piano at 9:23.

  It doesn't appear anywhere before Part IV.
- **Arpeggio (7:39.4).** When the key changes to seven sharps, the piano starts rising broken chords. They carry on to the finale, where the horns take them over at 8:47.
- **Finale (8:45).** The whole orchestra comes back in: the flutes' scale run from Part I, then the theme in full, with the horns playing the piano's arpeggios. The trumpets play short repeated notes (A A A A) that recall the call's head, but not the call itself. From 9:24 it thins out to strings and piano.
